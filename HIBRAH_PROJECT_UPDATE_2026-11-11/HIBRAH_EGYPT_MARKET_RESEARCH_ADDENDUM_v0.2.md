# 1. Purpose and status

This addendum consolidates the first-stage Egypt market research summary, the 123-row Google Maps directory, and the initial Facebook page-review observations supplied by the Founder on 2026-11-11. It does not replace the underlying research report or approve any marketing strategy, service, price, promise, or content.

The research also reported:
- Market is fragmented across ready-to-wear chains, rental, alterations, neighborhood tailors and appointment-based providers.
- Public prices for appointment-based tailoring were generally not visible on the major provider websites reviewed. This is not, by itself, a weakness or a recommendation to publish prices.
- Ready-to-wear prices observed in ads are not directly comparable with made-to-order tailoring.
- A customer question about what differentiates a lower-priced suit from a much more expensive one may indicate an educational opportunity; it does not prove an unmet market gap.
- Search visibility outside Cairo may overrepresent ready-to-wear, rental and occasionwear; this may be a search/access artifact, not proof of low tailoring demand.
- No competitor was contacted; no customer survey was completed; no competitor price was estimated; no HIBRAH price/margin calculation or viable segment conclusion was established.

# 3. Directory audit — directly inspected

The CSV contains:
- **123 rows**
- **98 distinct listed business names**
- **27 distinct `search_query_area` labels**
- **123 distinct Google Maps place IDs**
- **5 rows without a Maps rating and review-count value**

Important interpretation:
1. The directory is a list of Maps records/locations, not a count of unique companies. Repeated brand names often represent separate branches.
2. `search_query_area` is the area used to discover a result. It is not a verified physical location. Use `address_as_listed` as the address evidence, then independently validate the address before geographic analysis or contact.
3. The report summary says 30 areas were searched; the retained CSV currently represents 27 distinct search-area labels. This may be explained by areas with no retained results, but the discrepancy should be documented rather than silently erased.
4. Some records show apparent query-area/address mismatches and need verification. Examples include:
   - Catalonia listed under Banha while its address text points to Shibin El Kom, Menoufia.
   - Vialle listed under Kafr El Sheikh while the address contains a governorate label that appears inconsistent.
   - TiE HOUSE and Maurice Hanna entries listed under Fayoum whose address text points to Cairo.
   - A TiE HOUSE entry listed under Hurghada whose address text points to Luxor.
5. Do not use rating/review count as quality proof. Ratings are platform signals, may be stale, and do not reveal garment quality or tailoring method.
6. Do not classify every listed menswear store as a direct tailoring competitor. Record the actual service type only when the listing/page establishes it.

## 4.1 Strategic guardrails

- Do not change HIBRAH's private-price policy because comments ask for prices. Improve the clarity and usefulness of private responses instead.
- Do not publish or promise a customer-supplied-fabric service until operations, workshop acceptance, pricing, inspection, defect and responsibility terms are verified and approved.
- Do not advertise a delivery-time advantage. HIBRAH's own cycle time and service promise remain unverified and unapproved.
- Do not claim competitors have weak engagement based only on low visible comment counts.
- Do not infer that educational content is an unmet market gap from low comment counts or one Reddit thread.
- Do not treat competitor recommendation percentages as independent quality audits.

# 5. Current evidence-based hypotheses to test

| Hypothesis | Why it is plausible | Evidence needed before decision |
|---|---|---|
| Prospective buyers want early clarity on price and location/service access | Practical questions appeared in the small Facebook sample; price visibility is limited on some tailoring sites | Larger dated comment sample, customer interviews, HIBRAH inquiry records |
| Some buyers may want to supply their own fabric | One observed question | Repeated customer demand plus written operational feasibility and cost model |
| Delivery-time expectations may influence choice | One old complaint | Multiple current examples, interview evidence, competitor process data; separately validate HIBRAH capacity |
| Educational content about suit value and fabric may help buyers evaluate providers | One observed discussion topic and broad product complexity | Content tests measuring qualified inquiries, not engagement alone |

These are research hypotheses, not approved positioning or content pillars.

# 6. Research limitations and missing evidence

- The first-stage Facebook and Instagram work was largely logged-out and incomplete.
- Facebook group/search access and some review tabs were unavailable.
- Instagram profile-level inspection did not include a full grid/post/comment/Story/Highlight audit.
- No competitor mystery-shop inquiry was conducted.
- No direct customer interviews or representative survey were conducted.
- Google Maps records require address and category verification.
- No market-size, share, demand-volume or reliable competitor-pricing conclusion is supported.
- Raw screenshots, post URLs, observation log and the full report must be retained alongside the final evidence register.

# 8. HIBRAH implications — recommendations only

1. Preserve the current decision not to publish prices. Develop a clear private response that explains what determines the quote, once the approved price process is confirmed.
2. Treat customer-supplied fabric as an operational validation question, not a service launch.
3. Build HIBRAH's own delivery-time evidence from actual orders before communicating a timeframe.
4. Start collecting HIBRAH's own genuine customer feedback and process evidence only after real customer work exists and consent is secured.
5. Prioritize accurate service explanation and trust-building over competitor comparisons or unsupported superiority claims.
