## 2. Business objective and priority

The immediate objective is to help HIBRAH identify and test a realistic route to its **first paying customer**, while preserving the potential to build repeat customer relationships.

Research must support a practical decision; desk research alone cannot prove commercial viability.

Priority order:
1. **Acquisition decision research:** candidate customer needs, segments, triggers, alternatives, reachable audiences, messages and channels that merit a small test.
2. **Commercial and customer intelligence:** competitor/service/price comparison, customer journey, objections, language, content and financial measurement inputs.
3. **Minimum viable research infrastructure:** source register, evidence records, competitor database, quality checks and reusable reporting templates.

Do not expand scope merely to maximize collected data.

## 5. Evidence and source rules

Classify every material finding as:
- **Direct observation:** content/information directly inspected in the cited source.
- **Source-reported claim:** statement made by a business, customer, platform or publisher but not independently verified.
- **Third-party report:** claim published by a source other than the subject.
- **Interpretation:** reasoned reading of evidence.
- **Hypothesis:** testable proposition not yet established.
- **Unknown / inaccessible:** not found, not accessible or not verified.

Each evidence record should include where applicable: evidence ID; entity/competitor ID; exact source URL or platform locator; source type and publisher/account; access date and publication date; exact observation or short quotation; evidence classification; relevant workstream; confidence and verification status; and limitations or conflicting evidence.

Rules:
1. Preserve original source links and exact observations; do not rely solely on model summaries.
2. Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
3. Do not infer sales from views, likes, followers or comments.
4. Do not estimate unpublished prices.
5. Do not treat a search-area label as a business's verified service location.
6. Do not generalize from a single customer comment.
7. Record source dates; refresh time-sensitive prices and availability before relying on them.
8. Never invent contact details, customer quotations, fabric specifications or workshop capabilities.
9. Do not treat an inferred social profile or website link as verified without checking it.
10. Respect platform access controls, privacy and applicable terms. Use only authorized sessions and accessible information.

## 7. Recommended technical execution architecture

### Recommendation: Lean, evidence-first architecture (Option A)

Start with a human-supervised workflow using tools already available to the Founder. Do not purchase research subscriptions or build broad automation before a pilot proves the need.

**Tool/model roles**
- **ChatGPT — lead research and workflow model:** convert the requirements into research plans/prompts; perform public-source discovery where available; structure observations; compare evidence; draft analysis and reports.
- **Authorized browser session:** inspect accessible HIBRAH Facebook/Instagram content and interactions when the connected browser/session is available. If unavailable or blocked, record the limitation and ask the Founder to restore access; do not bypass login or access controls.
- **Google Sheets or Excel — evidence system:** maintain competitor database, source register, pricing records and findings with stable IDs and source URLs.
- **Claude — optional independent reviewer:** review selected high-impact findings, reasoning, contradictions and unsupported conclusions. Claude is not a source of truth; factual claims must trace to original evidence.
- **Perplexity — optional source-discovery aid:** use only if it materially improves discovery of public sources. Do not duplicate every search across models or treat its answer as evidence without opening original sources.

Check model availability, browsing capability, limits and account entitlements at execution time. Do not assume a particular subscription or feature is available.

### Architecture alternatives

**Option A — Lean manual-assisted (recommended now)**
- ChatGPT + authorized browser + Sheets/Excel + human verification.
- Lowest incremental cost and easiest to audit.
- More manual work; limited scale and platform coverage.

**Option B — Multi-model review**
- Option A plus selective Claude review and optional Perplexity source discovery.
- Useful for adversarial review and finding missed sources.
- More time and possible duplication; no automatic guarantee of better accuracy.
- Use selectively, not as parallel mass research.

**Option C — Expanded automation (defer)**
- Structured database, permitted data exports/APIs, repeatable ingestion and automated quality checks.
- More repeatable at scale but requires setup, maintenance, access validation and possibly paid services.
- Not justified until the pilot demonstrates recurring workload and clear return.

### Pilot before full execution

Run a bounded pilot on a small, representative sample of competitors and sources. Test:
1. Can the source be accessed lawfully and consistently?
2. Can required fields be captured with a direct source reference?
3. Can a second reviewer reproduce the observation?
4. How much human time does each verified record require?
5. Which source types provide the most useful evidence for the acquisition decision?
6. Does authorized Facebook/Instagram access expose the content/interactions needed, or are there platform limitations?

After the pilot, Development & R&D recommends whether to retain Option A, add selective Option B review, or defer/justify automation. Any paid tool or material access requirement returns to the Founder for approval.
