# HIBRAH — Brand Foundation

## 1. Purpose and authority

This document is the shared reference for HIBRAH's brand identity and market-facing direction. Every function must understand and apply it. Functions may provide evidence-based recommendations for improvement, but must not independently redefine the brand.

The Founder retains final authority over brand identity, positioning, strategic direction, customer policies, pricing, and material external commitments. Changes to this document require Founder approval.

## 2. Brand identity

- Arabic brand name: **حِبَرَة** (kasra under ح and fatha over ب; preserve this exact spelling and diacritic treatment wherever the Arabic brand name is written).
- English brand name: **HIBRAH**
- English name: **HIBRAH**
- Business: men's bespoke / made-to-measure tailoring in Egypt, with men's suits as the primary product.
- Long-term direction: a tailoring workshop that evolves and uses contemporary tools to design and make refined suits. This is a direction, not a claim that HIBRAH currently owns or operates a dedicated workshop.
- Primary slogan: **حُسْنٌ يُرْتَدَى** (always preserve the supplied diacritics when the slogan is used).
- The longer phrase previously recorded as a full slogan is not part of the default brand signature and should not be added automatically.

### Meaning of the slogan

**حُسْنٌ يُرْتَدَى** expresses the intention to create a considered, suitable and well-made garment for its wearer. It does not mean that the most expensive option is always the best option.

**فَصَّلْنَاهُ لِيَسُرَّكَ** expresses care for the customer's needs and experience before, during and after the order. The work should be shaped around the person who will wear the suit.

## 3. Positioning

HIBRAH is a calm, professional and honest tailoring brand offering well-considered men's suits at a balanced, respectable value: neither positioned as the cheapest option nor dependent on excessive luxury pricing.

The brand should communicate:
- Craft and attention to the garment.
- Honesty and clarity in recommendations, fabric descriptions, price and limitations.
- Professionalism and respect for the customer.
- A composed, understated sense of refinement.
- A practical relationship with the customer, not pressure to buy.

HIBRAH must not present itself as more established, luxurious, technically advanced or operationally capable than the evidence supports.

## 4. Values and behavioural standards

1. **Honesty first:** never exaggerate fabric, workmanship, service, technology, delivery, guarantees or results.
2. **Craftsmanship:** care about tangible construction, fit, finishing and the suitability of the garment.
3. **Responsibility:** acknowledge and handle problems rather than conceal them.
4. **Clarity:** explain choices, limitations, prices and next steps without ambiguity.
5. **Professionalism:** communicate respectfully, keep records and manage commitments carefully.
6. **Consistency and mastery:** learn from each order and improve the repeatability of the result.
7. **Customer respect:** recommend according to the customer's use and needs; do not push a more expensive option solely to increase revenue.

These values are commitments and decision criteria. They are not, by themselves, proof that every operational standard has already been achieved.

## 5. Product and service

### Primary product
Men's bespoke / made-to-measure suits.

### Fabric direction
Fabric is the central subject of the brand's ongoing editorial content; the suit is the product built around the fabric. Wool is the main commercial editorial emphasis, particularly English and Italian wool. Turkish fabric remains an available option but is not the permanent centre of the content direction; it may be featured for specific purposes, including a lower price tier.

Do not invent fabric composition, mill, origin, grade, performance, construction method or technical specification. Use only information verified for the actual fabric being offered.

### Service principles
The intended customer experience includes understanding the customer's intended use, helping select suitable fabric, discussing relevant suit details, taking measurements, coordinating tailoring, fitting, delivery and follow-up. Home visits may be used when practical and agreed. The exact availability and conditions of any service must be represented accurately.

## 6. Core customer

The primary customer is a man who:
- Has recurring or regular need for suits or formal wear.
- Appreciates fit, fabric, workmanship and a considered result.
- Values professional, clear and dependable interaction.
- May prefer an ongoing relationship over a one-time occasion purchase.

Potential occupational examples include legal professionals, doctors, banking and finance professionals, managers, business owners, academics and media professionals. Occupation alone does not establish customer fit; these are potential audience contexts, not exclusive eligibility criteria.

Customers with non-standard body proportions or difficulty finding suitable ready-to-wear clothing may be addressed through distinct content or campaigns. Occasion-led customers may also be served, but are not the central positioning.

## 7. Customer value and proof

Potential value areas include:
- Fabric selection based on intended use.
- Consideration of fit, construction, finishing and personal preferences.
- Clear guidance without imposing taste.
- A personal and professional ordering experience.
- Convenient appointment arrangements where operationally feasible.
- Retaining relevant measurements and preferences to support future orders, subject to appropriate handling of customer data.

The brand's differentiation should be demonstrated through the finished garment and the customer's actual experience. Do not turn an aspiration into a public claim before it is consistently delivered and evidenced.

## 8. Brand voice and communication

- Language: natural, clear Egyptian Arabic unless another language is requested.
- Tone: composed, direct, professional, understated and human.
- Avoid aggressive selling, exaggerated luxury clichés, forced friendliness and unsupported superlatives.
- Prefer concrete descriptions over abstract claims.
- Do not force a call to action into every piece of content.
- Public prices are not to be published unless specifically approved; price discussion is handled privately where appropriate.

Avoid the following customer-facing wording: "فلسفة", "البدلة الحلوة", "قصات", "على قد توقعاتك", "غايتنا", "حسن ظنك", "سعيد", and "راضي".

## 9. Visual identity and official logo use

- Core palette: brown and cream.
- Do not introduce olive/green or gold as brand colours.
- Arabic brand name in text: **حِبَرَة**; English brand name: **HIBRAH**.
- Use only the official logo files supplied by the Founder. The Founder intends to provide a transparent version and a standard version in a dedicated `Logo` folder.
- No HIBRAH design, post, story, visual, or other creative asset may be finalized or published without placing the official supplied logo in it.
- Do not recreate, redraw, approximate, typeset, generate, or substitute a logo. If the official files are not yet available, keep the design on hold until they are supplied.
- Keep the visual character quiet, refined and craft-led; avoid loud luxury signals.

### Hashtag rule

- Hashtags are optional, not a mandatory part of HIBRAH content.
- Marketing should recommend a hashtag only when there is a clear distribution or discoverability rationale. Do not add hashtags decoratively or by default.
- If a branded hashtag is used, preserve the exact Arabic brand spelling and diacritics: **#حِبَرَة**.

## 10. Channel and claim rules

Current primary social channels are Facebook and Instagram. WhatsApp, Messenger and phone are customer contact routes. A website inquiry/booking form is a future possibility, not a current channel unless implemented.

No function may publish or promise an unverified:
- Price, discount or payment exception.
- Delivery time or service area.
- Home visit availability or travel condition.
- Alteration policy, warranty or guarantee.
- Fabric specification, workshop capability or technical feature.
- Customer result, testimonial or performance claim.

## 11. Relationship with the other foundations

- This Brand Foundation defines **what HIBRAH is and how it should be represented**.
- The Operating Workflow defines **how work moves through HIBRAH**.
- The Organizational Structure defines **which functions and accountable owners perform that work and how authority/reporting are arranged**.

The Brand Foundation informs all functions. It does not replace workflow design or assign every operational task.
