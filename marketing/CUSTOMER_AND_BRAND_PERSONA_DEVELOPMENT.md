# HIBRAH — Customer and Brand Persona Development Framework

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the proposed working method for developing HIBRAH's Customer Persona(s) and Brand Persona. It is a process framework, not an approved persona profile or a change to the approved Brand Foundation.

The objective is to build useful, evidence-based profiles that guide positioning, services, customer experience, communication and content decisions.

## 2. Authority and source of truth

The approved `BRAND_FOUNDATION.md`, `OPERATING_WORKFLOW.md` and `ORGANIZATIONAL_STRUCTURE.md` remain authoritative.

This framework must not silently redefine the approved brand, customer direction, service commitments or organizational responsibilities. Any material change to the Brand Foundation requires Founder approval.

Current status:
- HIBRAH's approved Brand Foundation already defines a core customer direction.
- Existing research identifies possible customer segments as hypotheses, not validated personas.
- No completed, separately approved Customer Persona or Brand Persona is established by this document.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Customer Persona

An evidence-based representation of a meaningful customer type. It should explain relevant context, needs, buying situations, decision criteria, concerns, behaviour and the value the customer seeks from HIBRAH.

A persona is not a fictional biography. Demographics, occupation, income, lifestyle or preferences must not be invented to make a profile feel complete.

### 3.2 Brand Persona

A practical description of how HIBRAH should behave and communicate as a brand: its character, principles in action, relationship with customers, voice, language, emotional register and communication boundaries.

The Brand Persona must express the approved Brand Foundation; it does not replace or independently rewrite it.

## 4. Accountability and collaboration

### Marketing — Accountable owner

Marketing owns customer and market understanding and the marketing use of the personas. It is responsible for:
- Reviewing market, competitor, customer and sales evidence.
- Identifying and evaluating potential customer segments.
- Understanding customer needs, buying situations, decision criteria, questions and objections.
- Recommending the primary and any justified secondary customer personas.
- Translating validated customer understanding into positioning, value propositions, marketing messages and campaign/content implications.
- Recording evidence gaps and recommending further research.

### Creative — Contributing function for Brand Persona

Creative collaborates with Marketing to define how the approved brand identity is expressed in a recognisable and consistent character. It is responsible for contributing:
- Brand character and behavioural expression.
- Voice, language, tone and creative communication principles.
- Creative implications and examples across content formats.
- Checks for consistency between the proposed Brand Persona and the approved Brand Foundation.

Creative does not independently redefine marketing positioning or the approved brand identity.

### Sales & Customer Relations — Evidence contributor

Sales supplies actual customer questions, needs, preferences, objections, lead outcomes, purchase reasons where stated, and post-sale feedback. Customer statements must be distinguished from staff interpretation.

### Founder — Final authority

The Founder reviews and approves the resulting strategic direction and any material brand decisions. The Founder retains final authority over the Brand Foundation and customer-facing policy.

The GM coordinates dependencies and follow-up when required; the GM does not replace specialist ownership.

## 5. Evidence discipline

Every material statement must be classified as one of the following:

- **Evidence:** Directly supported by an accessible source, observed behaviour, recorded customer interaction or reliable data.
- **Inference:** A reasoned interpretation of evidence; the reasoning must be stated.
- **Hypothesis:** A plausible proposition that still needs validation.
- **Unknown:** Information not currently established.

For customer evidence:
- Separate customer-stated reasons from employee interpretation.
- Do not generalise from isolated comments or a small, biased sample.
- Do not treat public comments, search listings or competitor claims as proof of HIBRAH customer behaviour.
- Record source, date, sample/context and limitations where available.
- Do not invent quotations, customer stories, statistics or demographic detail.

## 6. Proposed development workflow

The work proceeds in controlled steps. Each step produces a reviewable output before the next step is treated as complete.

### Step 1 — Evidence and gap review

Marketing reviews the existing approved Brand Foundation, current market research, product/service research and any available Sales evidence.

Output:
1. What is currently known about customers, with sources.
2. Which customer segments have appeared in the research, clearly labelled as hypotheses unless validated.
3. Which customer questions cannot yet be answered.
4. What additional data is required, and how it could be collected.

Do not draft final personas at this stage.

### Step 2 — Segment evaluation

Marketing evaluates candidate segments against evidence of:
- Relevance to HIBRAH's approved product and positioning.
- Frequency and context of suit-purchase need.
- Customer needs and decision criteria.
- Fit with HIBRAH's actual and feasible service model.
- Evidence strength and accessibility.
- Commercial relevance, using real data where available.

Output: a reasoned segment assessment, including unresolved assumptions and evidence gaps.

### Step 3 — Customer Persona development

Marketing recommends one primary Customer Persona and only adds secondary personas where distinct needs and evidence justify them.

Each profile should cover only relevant, supportable dimensions:
- Customer context and suit-use situations.
- Needs and desired outcomes.
- Buying triggers and decision criteria.
- Questions, concerns and objections.
- Relevant service expectations.
- Evidence, inferences, hypotheses and unknowns.
- Implications for HIBRAH's offer and marketing.

Output: draft persona profile(s) with traceable evidence and confidence limitations.

### Step 4 — Brand Persona development

Marketing and Creative jointly translate the approved Brand Foundation into a practical Brand Persona.

The work should define:
- Core character and defining traits.
- How the brand behaves toward customers.
- Voice, tone and language principles.
- What the brand says and avoids saying.
- How the character appears in content and customer communication.
- Consistency checks against the approved Brand Foundation.

Output: a draft Brand Persona and practical communication examples.

### Step 5 — Service, value and content implications

Marketing uses the Customer Persona(s) to identify relevant customer value, service opportunities, messages and content themes. Creative develops the appropriate creative expression.

Potential services or features must be checked with the responsible function before being represented as available. Development & R&D assesses technical feasibility where relevant; Sales assesses customer-journey and delivery implications; Finance assesses cost and margin implications where relevant.

No unverified feature, service, policy, technical capability or customer benefit may be advertised as an existing HIBRAH capability.

Output: a prioritised set of recommendations, each labelled by evidence status and operational readiness.

### Step 6 — Review, validation and approval

Marketing and Creative review the drafts for evidence quality, usefulness and consistency. Sales checks whether the customer-facing assumptions reflect actual interactions. The Founder reviews the proposed strategic direction and approves any material brand or customer-policy decisions.

Personas remain drafts until the Founder approves them. New evidence may require later revision.

## 7. Initial known constraints

The following are established in the approved Brand Foundation:
- HIBRAH's primary product is men's bespoke / made-to-measure suits in Egypt.
- The core customer direction is a man with recurring or regular need for suits/formal wear who appreciates fit, fabric, workmanship and clear, dependable interaction.
- Potential occupational contexts are examples, not exclusive eligibility criteria.
- Customers with non-standard body proportions may be addressed through distinct content or campaigns.
- Occasion-led customers may be served but are not the central positioning.
- The brand voice is natural, clear Egyptian Arabic; composed, direct, professional, understated and human.
- HIBRAH must not claim capabilities or service conditions that have not been verified.

These points are a starting baseline, not a substitute for evidence review or completed personas.

## 8. Immediate next action

Begin with Step 1 only. Marketing prepares the four-part Evidence and Gap Review. The Founder reviews that output before the team proceeds to segment evaluation.
