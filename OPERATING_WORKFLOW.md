# HIBRAH — Operating Workflow

## 1. Purpose and authority

This document defines how work moves through HIBRAH: how goals become plans and tasks, how functions collaborate, how customer orders are handled, how information is exchanged, and how work is reviewed and reported.

The Founder retains final decision-making authority. The Founder may work directly with any function and is not required to route every instruction through the General Manager (GM).

This workflow is approved as the current operating baseline. It is intentionally practical and will be adjusted when actual work reveals a problem or a better method. Do not add procedures, reporting cycles or automation before they are useful.

## 2. Operating principles

1. Every task has one accountable primary owner.
2. Work is directed by approved company goals and priorities.
3. Functional owners may produce, decide and act within the approved plan and their responsibilities.
4. The Founder retains final authority and currently performs direct technical review until the relevant results and working standards are established.
5. The GM coordinates priorities and execution; the GM is not a mandatory gatekeeper for Founder-to-function communication.
6. Each function is responsible for the technical quality and completeness of its own work.
7. The workflow must reflect actual work. Do not create separate departments for activities that are currently responsibilities inside another function.
8. Facts, current practices, provisional operating details, assumptions and unknowns must remain distinguishable.
9. Customer-facing claims and commitments must match capabilities that have actually been verified.
10. Start with a workable minimum process; improve it in response to evidence and recurring needs.

## 3. From company goal to daily execution

### 3.1 Goal and strategy

1. The Founder sets or confirms the business goal and its strategic importance.
2. The relevant specialist function evaluates the goal and develops a strategy and plan within its professional remit.
3. The plan states the intended result, approach, workstreams, tasks, measures, resources, dependencies, risks and completion criteria.
4. The Founder reviews and approves the plan and any Founder-reserved decisions.
5. Once approved, the plan becomes the basis for function-level objectives and execution.

Example: a target of acquiring eight customers is a business goal, not a task list. Marketing assesses how the goal can be reached and proposes the strategy and campaign/content activities. After Founder approval, the GM sets the operating priorities and coordinates the functions' tasks.

### 3.2 GM priority-setting and task coordination

The GM:
- Translates approved goals and plans into priorities for the current period.
- Determines which workstreams need attention now and which should wait.
- Identifies the tasks required from each function and their dependencies.
- Assigns or confirms one accountable owner for each task.
- Sets practical sequencing and target dates.
- Checks progress, blockers, handoffs and completion.
- Reprioritises within the approved goals and plan when circumstances change.
- Escalates material changes, conflicts, resource implications or decisions outside delegated authority to the Founder.

The GM does not independently change the approved company goal, brand direction, strategy or Founder-reserved policy. If the priority change materially alters the approved plan, the GM presents it to the Founder for decision.

### 3.3 Daily execution cycle

At the start of a working period, the GM communicates:
- The current priority and why it matters.
- The tasks required from each function.
- The accountable owner for each task.
- Dependencies, expected outputs and target timing.
- Any blocker or decision required.

Each function executes its assigned work, provides necessary cross-functional inputs, and reports meaningful status changes or blockers. The GM coordinates the combined work and updates the Founder through the reporting/session method currently set by the Founder.

## 4. Initial brand, marketing and content workflow

### 4.1 Brand Foundation

The Founder and Marketing work together to establish and validate the Brand Foundation. Marketing identifies only the missing information or confirmations needed to make it usable. Once approved, all functions read and apply it as the common brand reference.

Other functions may identify evidence that suggests a change is needed, but do not independently redefine the brand.

### 4.2 Market and marketing strategy

Marketing continuously researches and evaluates:
- Customers and customer needs.
- Market conditions and demand.
- Competitors and alternatives.
- Pricing context and customer value perception.
- Relevant seasons, occasions and market opportunities.
- Acquisition, conversion and retention performance.

Marketing translates this understanding into marketing goals, strategy, channel choices, campaigns and measures. Media buying and paid campaign management sit within Marketing.

### 4.3 Content strategy and production

Marketing and Creative collaborate on content strategy and the content plan:
- Marketing owns the marketing objectives, audience, strategic direction and intended business outcome.
- Creative contributes creative research, content expertise, concepts and production judgement.
- Together they translate marketing objectives into content themes, formats, briefs, plans and evaluation criteria.
- Creative produces the approved content.
- Marketing selects and manages distribution channels according to the marketing strategy.
- Publication and customer response handling follow the customer-contact and sales workflow; publication approval remains subject to the Founder where required.

Creative is not a production-only function. It continuously researches competitors, references, trends, visual language, content formats, tools and relevant product knowledge. It builds and maintains a useful library of images, video, ideas, references and tested creative approaches. It shares relevant learning with Marketing and uses it to improve future work.

Marketing and Creative continuously evaluate content and market response and improve their respective work. Research findings must be distinguished from assumptions and creative interpretation.

## 5. Customer inquiry, sales, production and delivery workflow

**Sales & Customer Relations owns the complete customer journey**, from the first inquiry through production coordination, delivery and post-sale follow-up. There is no separate Customer Service or Production Coordination function in the current structure.

### 5.1 Inquiry and qualification

Customer contact may arrive through Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger or phone. Sales:
1. Receives and responds to the inquiry.
2. Understands the customer's need, intended use, timing and relevant preferences.
3. Answers questions accurately and clarifies what is not yet known.
4. Records the lead source and useful customer information.
5. Determines the appropriate next step and follows up.

Comments, messages and incoming questions are part of the customer contact process. Sales owns customer communication and the relationship.

### 5.2 Appointment, consultation and order definition

Sales:
1. Coordinates a suitable customer appointment.
2. Confirms whether a home visit or workshop meeting is practical for the customer's location and schedule.
3. Discusses fabric, suit details and customer preferences.
4. Takes and records measurements and the agreed order specifications.
5. Explains the applicable price and payment arrangement accurately.
6. Confirms the order in accordance with the current payment and order-confirmation practice.

Do not promise a home visit, location, delivery date, fitting schedule, alteration condition or other service term unless it is feasible and confirmed for that customer.

### 5.3 Workshop and production coordination

After the order is confirmed, Sales remains accountable for coordinating the order with the relevant fabric source, tailor or external workshop. Sales:
- Passes the agreed measurements, fabric and order specifications to the production provider.
- Confirms the next production milestone and fitting timing.
- Follows up on progress and expected completion.
- Coordinates collection, fitting appointments and required adjustments.
- Communicates delays or changes to the customer and escalates material issues.

Production is currently performed through external tailoring workshops/tailors according to the Founder-reported operating model. HIBRAH must not imply that it owns or directly operates a dedicated production workshop unless that changes.

### 5.4 Fitting, adjustment and delivery

Sales coordinates the fitting, gathers the customer's feedback, communicates required adjustments to the tailor/workshop, follows the adjustment through, and arranges final delivery.

The Founder currently performs direct technical review of results as a temporary control while the quality standard and repeatability are being established. The Founder may inspect or review any order or technical output. This does not transfer all production work to the Founder.

The previously described approximately 14-day order timeline is an operational target/expectation, not an unconditional public guarantee. Actual customer commitments must be checked against current capacity and the specific order.

### 5.5 Post-sale relationship and retention

Sales owns post-delivery customer contact, issue handling, feedback, review requests and ongoing customer relationship. Sales records customer feedback and repeat-purchase signals.

Marketing may design retention or reactivation campaigns for previous customers. Sales executes the direct customer communication and reports responses and outcomes to Marketing. Sales remains the owner of the customer relationship.

## 6. Marketing and Sales feedback loop

Sales captures and shares useful customer and sales intelligence with Marketing, including:
- Common questions and information gaps.
- Customer needs, use cases and preferences.
- Objections and concerns, including price, fabric, fit, trust and timing.
- Lead source and customer type.
- Lead quality and progression through the sales process.
- Conversion outcome: purchased, lost, deferred or not qualified.
- Customer-stated reasons for decisions, separated from staff interpretation.
- Time from inquiry to conversion, appointment timing and order/delivery timeline.
- Post-sale feedback, repeat interest and recurring service issues.

Marketing compares campaign and content performance with actual sales outcomes. It uses the combined evidence to improve targeting, messaging, content, campaigns and acquisition strategy.

Accountability:
- Sales owns accurate customer interaction, pipeline, outcome and feedback capture.
- Marketing owns marketing analysis and the resulting marketing recommendations.
- GM ensures the information exchange and agreed follow-up are not lost.
- The Founder decides matters reserved to the Founder.

Do not infer customer motives or generalise from isolated anecdotes. Do not fabricate testimonials, quotations or conversion explanations.

## 7. Marketing and Finance workflow

Finance records and analyses actual business finances, including:
- Revenue and receipts.
- Fabric and material costs.
- Tailor/workshop costs.
- Transport and customer-visit costs.
- Marketing, creative, media-buying and advertising costs.
- Other costs attributable to an order or business activity.
- Cash position, payment status and order-level profitability.

Marketing and Finance collaborate to examine market and competitor pricing, HIBRAH's actual costs, price acceptability and margin. They develop and maintain practical cost/pricing calculations and financial tracking.

Finance's pricing and discount review/approval role becomes operational as real customer transactions begin and sufficient cost/revenue data exists. The Founder retains final authority over pricing and exceptional discounts unless a specific delegated authority is later recorded.

Campaigns are reviewed after completion using campaign expenditure and performance alongside lead, conversion and sales information. Finance supplies reliable cost/revenue data; Marketing analyses marketing effectiveness; GM coordinates the review and follow-up.

## 8. Marketing and Development & R&D workflow

Development & R&D continuously researches and tests opportunities to improve:
- Suit, fabric, pattern and tailoring knowledge or methods.
- Product quality and technical problem-solving.
- Order and workshop processes and efficiency.
- Measurement records, pattern data and relevant customer/order information.
- AI, automation, software, tools and professional skills.
- New technical capabilities or service possibilities.

Development shares verified findings, limitations and test results with Marketing. Marketing shares customer needs, market signals and potential value opportunities with Development.

The two functions assess whether a development solves a real customer problem and whether it creates a genuine customer benefit or communication opportunity. No feature, technical capability or benefit may be claimed publicly until it exists, has been tested sufficiently for the claim, and is approved for communication where required.

Development remains a distinct specialty; Marketing does not certify technical feasibility, and Development does not independently set brand or marketing strategy.

## 9. Finance and Sales information exchange

Sales provides Finance with complete and timely order information needed to record:
- Agreed order value and payment schedule.
- Deposits, instalments, balances and receipts.
- Order-specific fabric, tailoring, transport and other costs.
- Refunds, adjustments, discounts or exceptional arrangements.
- Order completion and delivery status where relevant to financial records.

Finance maintains the financial record and raises missing, inconsistent or unusual entries with Sales and GM. Sales remains accountable for the customer relationship and operational order coordination; Finance remains accountable for financial recording and analysis.

## 10. Founder review, reporting and meetings

### 10.1 Founder authority and temporary technical review

The Founder:
- Makes final business decisions.
- Approves goals, strategic plans and matters reserved to the Founder.
- May work directly with any function.
- Currently performs direct technical review until results, standards and reliable execution are sufficiently established.

Functions are expected to produce and act within their approved responsibilities; the Founder is not expected to produce every deliverable personally. The temporary direct review arrangement may be revised by the Founder as the operating system matures.

### 10.2 Reports and review sessions

Each function is responsible for preparing its own status and performance information. The GM collects the function-level reports, follows up on actions and presents a consolidated operating picture to the Founder.

At the current stage:
- The Founder sets when review/reporting sessions take place.
- No fixed daily, weekly or monthly reporting cadence is imposed by this document.
- Reporting formats should be kept proportionate to the work and evidence available.

Later, the Founder and GM may establish regular reporting times and automate suitable parts once the workflow is stable.

### 10.3 GM's reporting responsibility

The GM does not write specialist reports on behalf of functions. The GM:
- Requests and collects each function's own report.
- Checks that owners, status, blockers and next actions are clear.
- Consolidates cross-functional priorities, dependencies, risks and decisions needed.
- Reports the combined picture to the Founder.
- Tracks actions after the Founder/GM review.

## 11. Task records and minimum handoff information

A task or handoff should contain only the information needed to execute reliably:
- Desired outcome and reason.
- One accountable owner.
- Supporting function(s), if any.
- Required input/source material.
- Deliverable and completion criteria.
- Priority and target timing.
- Dependencies or decisions needed.
- Status, blocker and next action.
- Relevant customer/order/campaign reference where appropriate.

Use existing records or lightweight trackers where possible. Do not create duplicate systems without a clear need.

## 12. Approval boundaries

The Founder retains final authority over:
- Business goals and strategy.
- Brand identity and positioning.
- Pricing policy, exceptional discounts and material financial commitments.
- Budgets and material spending decisions.
- Customer policies and exceptional commitments.
- Public claims or communications that require Founder approval.
- Material changes to the operating model or organizational authority.
- Approval and revision of the foundational project documents.

The GM may set operating priorities and coordinate tasks within approved goals and plans. Functional owners may make routine technical and execution decisions within their remit and approved constraints. Any matter outside those boundaries is escalated to the Founder.

## 13. Operating maturity and revision

This workflow is the approved starting system, not a claim that every process is already automated, fully staffed or proven at scale. The Founder is currently establishing each step directly. Improve the workflow when an actual issue, repeated friction, new capability or material change justifies it.
