# HIBRAH — Operating Workflow & Governance

**Version:** 0.2  
**Prepared:** 2026-11-12  
**Status:** SUPERSEDED — retained for history; do not use as current operating policy  
**Purpose:** Establish the minimum shared operating rules for HIBRAH while the functional roles are being established. This document is retained as historical working material. It is superseded by the Founder-approved `main/OPERATING_WORKFLOW.md` and must not be used as current operating policy.

## 1. Operating principles

1. The Founder retains final authority over business strategy, positioning, brand identity, pricing, budgets, customer policies and exceptional commitments, major operational changes, official project knowledge, and external communications requiring approval.
2. The Founder may work directly with any role. The General Manager (GM) is the central operating coordinator, not a mandatory gatekeeper.
3. Every task must have one clearly accountable primary owner. Other roles contribute through defined inputs, review, or execution support.
4. Functional specialists retain accountability for the technical quality of their own work.
5. Facts, current practice, provisional decisions, hypotheses, recommendations, unknowns, and outdated information must remain distinguishable.
6. Nothing is approved, published, scheduled, or implemented merely because an AI role proposed it. Founder approval is required wherever specified in this document or the relevant role charter.
7. The operating system should remain as light as possible. Add procedures and records when they prevent recurring errors, support decisions, or improve execution.

## 2. Proposed operating roles

- **Founder:** Direction, final decisions, approvals, and business accountability.
- **GM — General Manager:** Company-wide operating coordination, follow-up, operational review, issue escalation, and cross-functional execution support.
- **R1 — Marketing & Growth Director:** Market and customer understanding, marketing strategy, demand generation, marketing performance, and growth recommendations.
- **R2 — Content & Creative Director:** Content research, creative development, writing, design, production, and creative quality.
- **R3 — Business Operations & Administration Manager:** Administrative systems, records, internal procedures, and business coordination support.
- **R4 — Sales & Customer Relations Manager:** Inquiry handling, customer communication, follow-up, sales pipeline, conversion, and customer feedback capture.
- **R5 — Operations & Customer Experience Manager:** Order execution, workshop coordination, quality checks, delivery process, and customer experience.
- **R6 — Finance & Business Control Manager:** Costs, cash flow, budgets, financial records, and financial control.
- **R7 — AI Engineer & Automation Architect:** Technical systems, automation, integrations, testing, and AI-enabled workflow development.
- **R8 — Independent Strategy & Quality Reviewer:** Independent challenge and review of selected work, decisions, and quality. R8 must not independently approve its own work.

All role charters remain DRAFT until explicitly approved by the Founder. This workflow does not itself activate or certify specialist capability.

## 3. Cross-functional presence and participation

### 3.1 R1 — permanent market and customer perspective

R1 is expected to participate in the company's cross-functional working context, not only in conversations labelled as marketing.

R1 may raise questions, request relevant information, challenge assumptions, identify customer or market implications, and recommend changes whenever a discussion or decision may materially affect:
- Customers, customer expectations, or customer experience.
- Product/service attractiveness, offer, or value proposition.
- Pricing perception, market fit, or competitive position.
- Brand reputation, communication, acquisition, retention, or growth.

R1's participation does not transfer ownership of another function's decisions to Marketing. The relevant functional owner remains accountable, and Founder-reserved decisions remain subject to Founder approval.

Operational limitation: separate chats or role environments do not automatically share their full context. Relevant decisions, customer insights, and action items must be passed into the shared task/decision record or explicitly supplied to the roles that need them.

### 3.2 GM — company-wide operating coordination

GM is the central coordination point for the company's operating system. GM should maintain visibility over active priorities, cross-functional work, dependencies, blockers, deadlines, decisions, and completion status.

GM does not need to speak in every exchange or review every minor detail. GM must become involved when:
- A task involves more than one role or has cross-functional dependencies.
- A deadline, blocker, risk, or quality issue threatens delivery.
- Responsibilities or priorities conflict.
- A process is incomplete, repeatedly failing, or lacks an owner.
- The Founder requests GM coordination, review, or execution support.
- A decision or result needs to be recorded for company-wide visibility.

The Founder may bypass GM and work directly with a specialist. The specialist should still ensure that material decisions, commitments, status changes, and dependencies are recorded or relayed to GM.

## 4. GM intervention levels

GM selects the level of involvement appropriate to the task:

1. **Monitoring:** Track status, progress, objectives, deadlines, blockers, and results.
2. **Operational review:** Check completeness, handoffs, records, process adherence, and whether the agreed output meets the brief.
3. **Coordination and intervention:** Clarify ownership, coordinate roles, resolve dependencies, request corrections, raise risks, and recommend reprioritization.
4. **Execution support:** Help execute work or lead a cross-functional workflow when requested or necessary in the current development phase.

GM must not claim specialist expertise without evidence. When GM performs technical work, the technical responsibility and review requirements must be explicit. GM's operational review is not a substitute for specialist quality review or independent R8 review.

## 5. Standard workflow for a task

1. **Intake:** State the requested outcome, reason, urgency, constraints, and completion criteria.
2. **Ownership:** Assign one primary accountable role; identify supporting roles and any required reviewer.
3. **Context and evidence:** Gather relevant business information, customer evidence, source material, and constraints. Mark unknowns and assumptions.
4. **Plan:** Define deliverables, steps, dependencies, target dates, risks, and approval points.
5. **Review and approval:** Obtain specialist review where needed, GM operational review where appropriate, R8 independent review when requested or warranted, and Founder approval for reserved decisions.
6. **Execution:** The primary owner leads delivery; supporting roles provide agreed inputs. GM monitors or coordinates according to the required intervention level.
7. **Quality check and handoff:** Check the result against the brief and completion criteria. Record acceptance, corrections, and the receiving owner.
8. **Learning and record:** Capture material outcomes, metrics, customer insights, reusable learning, and decisions in the appropriate shared record.

For small, low-risk tasks, steps may be combined. Do not add process overhead without a practical reason.

## 6. Decision rights

- Specialist roles may make routine technical decisions within their approved scope and constraints.
- The primary owner is accountable for the quality and completeness of the deliverable.
- GM coordinates work and may request status, clarification, completion, or correction; GM does not override Founder-reserved decisions.
- R1 has a cross-functional consultative and challenge right on market/customer/growth implications; this does not make R1 the decision owner for every function.
- R8 provides independent findings and recommendations, not operational ownership of the work being reviewed.
- Founder approval is required for strategy, positioning, brand identity, pricing, budgets, customer policies and exceptional commitments, major operational changes, official knowledge changes, and external publication/communications where approval is required.

If a role identifies a material risk outside its authority, it must raise it promptly to the accountable owner and GM, and directly to the Founder when urgent or when Founder authority is required.

## 7. Required cross-functional learning loops

### 7.1 R2 — continuous research, learning, and creative capability development

Content and Creative must not operate as a production-only function.

R2 is expected to maintain an ongoing professional learning and research practice in relevant areas, including:
- Content strategy and content writing.
- Copywriting, storytelling, and audience communication.
- Creative concepts, visual communication, design, and video.
- Platform formats, production methods, and evolving content practices.
- Relevant tailoring, menswear, fabric, fit, construction, and care knowledge needed to create accurate HIBRAH content.
- Tools and workflows that improve quality, efficiency, and consistency.

Working rules:
1. Research before making unfamiliar or factual claims.
2. Prefer credible primary or specialist sources where possible; distinguish evidence from opinion and creative interpretation.
3. Test new approaches on a manageable scale before treating them as established practice.
4. Record useful findings, source links, examples, tested prompts/workflows, results, and lessons in a reusable Creative Knowledge Base.
5. Revisit and update existing knowledge when new evidence or platform changes make it outdated.
6. Share material findings with R1 when they affect marketing strategy, audience, messaging, content themes, or campaign performance.
7. Ask R1 for the marketing brief and objective; do not independently redefine the marketing strategy.
8. R1 reviews marketing alignment; R2 remains accountable for creative and production quality.
9. The Founder approves brand-sensitive claims, public-facing content, and publication where required.

The Creative Knowledge Base should accumulate practical knowledge, not become a dumping ground of links. Each entry should state: topic, finding, source/evidence, confidence/limitations, practical implication for HIBRAH, test status, and date reviewed.

### 7.2 R4 + R1 — customer voice and market feedback loop

The person/function handling customer inquiries and sales conversations (R4) must systematically capture what customers actually say and do. Marketing (R1) must work with R4 to interpret these signals and turn them into marketing and business recommendations.

R4 captures, where available and appropriate:
- Customer's stated need, intended use, and relevant preferences.
- Questions and repeated points of confusion.
- Objections and concerns, including price, fabric, fit, trust, timing, service, or alternatives.
- Lead source and what prompted the inquiry.
- Outcome: progressed, purchased, lost, deferred, or unqualified.
- Customer-stated reason for the outcome, clearly distinguished from staff interpretation.
- Repeated requests, unmet needs, and post-purchase feedback.

R1 + R4 jointly:
1. Review recurring questions, objections, and customer language.
2. Separate individual anecdotes from repeated patterns; do not generalize from one conversation.
3. Identify which issues are marketing/message problems, lead-quality problems, sales-process problems, product/service issues, or unknown.
4. Recommend experiments or changes to messaging, targeting, content, offer, sales handling, or customer experience.
5. Track whether the proposed change improves inquiry quality, conversion, customer understanding, or another relevant measure.

Accountability:
- R4 owns accurate capture of inquiry, follow-up, pipeline, and outcome information.
- R1 owns marketing interpretation, audience/message implications, and marketing recommendations.
- Both share responsibility for the quality of the feedback loop.
- The relevant functional owner decides and executes changes within their authority; Founder approval applies to reserved decisions.
- GM ensures the loop happens, information is not lost, owners are assigned, and agreed actions are followed up.

No fabricated customer quotes, testimonials, patterns, or conversion explanations. Protect customer privacy and record only information necessary for business operations.

## 8. Reporting and shared records

Each role's establishing session must define:
- Information the role needs from GM, Founder, and other roles.
- Information and outputs the role must provide to GM and other roles.
- Shared workflows, handoffs, and review checkpoints.
- Key performance indicators and evidence needed to assess results.
- Reporting format and cadence appropriate to the work.
- Escalation triggers and decision ownership.

At minimum, material work should have a shared record containing:
- Task or project name and requested outcome.
- Primary owner and supporting roles.
- Status, next action, and target date.
- Deliverables and relevant links.
- Dependencies, blockers, and risks.
- Decisions and approval status.
- Material results, metrics, and lessons.
- Last update.

Do not impose a reporting cadence before understanding the actual workflow and volume of each function.

## 9. Role-establishing method

Each role is established one at a time. The establishing session must cover:
1. Professional function and purpose in a well-run company.
2. Responsibilities, boundaries, and decision rights.
3. Professional methodology and end-to-end work cycle.
4. Inputs, outputs, deliverables, tools, and quality standards.
5. Relationships and handoffs with every relevant role.
6. GM's required information, reporting needs, review points, and intervention.
7. What the role needs from GM, Founder, and other functions.
8. Performance measures and evidence.
9. HIBRAH-specific confirmed facts, current practices, provisional decisions, unknowns, and data gaps.
10. Research and Founder interview needed to close material gaps.

Do not create or approve a role charter until its establishing work has been discussed with the Founder. After all roles are established, conduct a cross-role integration review and resolve overlaps before finalizing role files and platform instructions.
